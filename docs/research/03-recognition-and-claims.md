# How Cordia Classroom Can Earn Credible Recognition: Exam Bodies, Edtech Certifications, Evidence, and Claims (November 2026)

The most realistic recognition path for Cordia right now is not an official exam-body partnership. It is a cheap, stackable set of design and privacy credentials, followed by a small, honestly reported efficacy study. In order, that means a published logic model and research basis, the Digital Promise Research-Based Design (ESSA Tier 4) certification at $650, a free Common Sense Privacy evaluation, a completed HECVAT 4 (free), and a pre-registered study run with a University of South Alabama faculty partner. With about 77 accounts, 8 weekly actives and steep day-1 drop-off, retention is the bottleneck for both evidence and recognition. No credential will fix it, and no rigorous study can run until it improves.

## TL;DR

- **Official exam-body "partnerships" are mostly content licenses, not endorsements, and none is needed to sell prep.** NCSBN says it "does not recommend or endorse any review courses." LSAC, NCBE, ETS, AAMC and AICPA license official questions under paid terms, such as LSAC's $38 per student coaching license or ETS's $225–$450 per question. Cordia can legally build NCLEX or bar study tools now if it uses exam names only as descriptive fair use, with a non-affiliation disclaimer.
- **Best near-term recognition for the money:** Digital Promise Research-Based Design: ESSA Tier 4 ($650), a free Common Sense Privacy Seal evaluation, and a completed HECVAT 4 (free, often required in university procurement). Later options are the ISTE Seal ($5,000 Stage 1 review) and Digital Promise Responsibly Designed AI. 1EdTech TrustEd Apps requires paid membership. The Student Privacy Pledge was retired in April 2025.
- **Claims must track evidence.** "Research-based design" (citing spacing and retrieval research) is defensible today. "Proven to raise scores," pass rates, and "guaranteed" or "zero hallucination" claims need competent and reliable evidence first. FTC orders against DoNotPay (2025), Workado (2025) and Illuminate Education (2025–26) show the agency enforces exactly this against AI and edtech companies.

**What this means.** For every exam, "official" recognition is either (a) an exclusive deal (College Board with Khan Academy) or (b) a paid content license that permits *using* official questions and never implies endorsement. None is a seal saying the exam body vouches for a vendor. NCSBN goes further and says outright that it endorses no one.

**Live or recalled exam content.** The exam owners assert exclusive rights to exam content. The AAMC, for example, says it is "the exclusive owner of all rights in the MCAT exam," and NCBE, LSAC and ETS restrict reuse to licensed released items. For Cordia, the zero-hallucination design should be extended into a **content-provenance rule**: never generate, accept or store "recalled" live questions (for example, from user-pasted forum dumps), and generate exam-style items only from course material or licensed and public blueprints. This is an inference from the ownership terms above, not a quote from any single policy.

**Popular belief not supported by the evidence: "You need an official partnership to sell exam prep."** False. UWorld sells NCLEX-RN access directly to students ($169 for 60 days up to $449 for 730 days), and Archer Review's FAQ says its "30-day NCLEX-RN Q-Bank & Video Course Combo starts from $99" (its pass-guarantee Sure PASS plan starts at $159 for one month). Neither holds an NCSBN endorsement, because none exists. Jack Westin sells MCAT prep built around AAMC products without reproducing them. Licenses matter only if you want to *use official questions*.

### 3. Evidence standards

- **ESSA tiers** (federal definitions, summarized by IES REL Central in 2025). Tier 1 is strong (RCT). Tier 2 is moderate (quasi-experimental). Tier 3 is promising (a correlational study "comparing outcomes for a treatment group and a comparison group" with "statistical controls for selection bias"). Tier 4 "demonstrates a rationale" through "a well-specified logic model" plus ongoing study. Tiers 1–2 also require a large, multisite sample and a statistically significant positive outcome. Evidence strength: strong (statutory).
- **Funding consequence.** Tier 4 does not satisfy Section 1003 school-improvement money, which needs Tiers 1–3. This matters for K-12, much less for college sales.
- **Market reality.** LearnPlatform by Instructure's March 2023 analysis of the 100 most-accessed K-12 tools found only 26 had ESSA-aligned research, and "of the 26 solutions with published, ESSA-aligned research, only nine have studies with promising (ESSA Level III) or above"; its 2025 EdTech Top 40 found 45% of tools had published ESSA research, up from 32% in 2024. LearnPlatform's June 2024 release adds that "school districts are increasingly seeking ESSA-aligned research as part of their vetting processes to make evidence-based decisions." Evidence is a differentiator, not yet table stakes, and the bar is low.
- **Higher education, nursing and law.** ESSA does not formally govern colleges. In this research, procurement gates at universities were security, privacy and accessibility (HECVAT, SOC 2, VPAT), not efficacy tiers. Nursing programs buy into an established ecosystem (ATI) whose value proposition is NCLEX readiness, and they are judged on graduate NCLEX pass rates. Credible nursing evidence for Cordia is therefore exam-aligned outcomes such as course exam scores, ATI proctored scores, or later NCLEX pass status. I did not find a published faculty-side evidence standard for law schools; bar-prep buyers focus on licensed NCBE content. Evidence strength: moderate (inferred from procurement and fee documents, not surveys of decision-makers).
- **What Works Clearinghouse / Evidence for ESSA.** These are the formal K-12 review standards that Tier 1–2 designs must meet ("meets WWC standards with/without reservations"). They are realistic for Cordia only years out.

### 4. How study tools get adopted

- **Who decides.** For nursing, the program or faculty select an assessment and review ecosystem, and students pay through fees. Examples:
  - UNC Charlotte: ATI "tend to be $230 per semester."
  - University of Louisville: $728.75 charged in two semesters.
  - Regis College and Notre Dame of Maryland: $3,300 per program.
  - Isothermal CC: a one-time $1,075 "included with NUR 111 tuition."
  - Indiana University: ATI is "required for all students."
  
  For any campus purchase or pilot, IT security review (HECVAT/SOC 2) and accessibility review (VPAT) gate the deal, even at the pilot stage (Montclair State).
- **Pricing models.** There are three:
  - **Student-pays-via-program-fee** (ATI): roughly $150–$825 per semester, or $1,000–$3,300 per program.
  - **Direct-to-student** (UWorld, Archer).
  - **Donor or department subsidy**: Washburn raised gifts to give seniors $60 toward UWorld's $199 price, plus a $20 group discount.
  
  Cordia's $29 per semester is an order of magnitude below ATI's fee. That makes it an easy add-on or subsidy, not a replacement.
- **LMS integration.** Instructure's partner program has a **free tier**. Free-tier partners "are not permitted to access or use the API" and must pay for API access; paid fees are "specified in the applicable Order Form" and not public. Discovery in the Canvas Apps page runs through the EdCo Partner Portal, and Instructure tells vendors to "Upgrade to LTI 1.3." Partners must hold ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, or pass Instructure's security assessment. AI products in the Instructure Marketplace fall under an AI addendum that bans training on Instructure data and requires 72-hour AI-incident reporting. **Implication:** Cordia's current student-token Canvas access is a different channel from an institutional LTI 1.3 integration. Institutional adoption will eventually require LTI 1.3 and a security credential.
- **How competitors entered.** These patterns come from sources above:
  - Princeton Review bought NCSBN's own course.
  - AdaptiBar, JD Advising and Themis+UWorld built on NCBE-licensed questions.
  - Helix entered via AccessLex's study-aid partnership with NCBE.
  - UWorld and Archer went direct-to-student with question banks, then school cohorts.
  - Khan Academy won the College Board's exclusive.
  
  I did not verify entry histories for Quizlet, Anki tools, Knowt, StudyFetch, Brainscape, Amboss, Kaplan or BarBri in this research. Brainscape, for example, publishes NCLEX content with an NCSBN non-endorsement disclaimer.

### 5. Running a credible efficacy study with a small user base

Methodological guidance in this section is standard research practice rather than sourced statistics. Evidence strength for the learning-science premise (spacing and retrieval practice outperform restudy) is strong and replicated, though the core literature predates 2025.

- **Logic model first.** Inputs (course materials, Claude-generated, source-verified items) lead to activities (recall-only mastery, Leitner spacing, format-switched retries, confidence ratings). Those produce short-term outputs (retrieval attempts, spaced sessions), then intermediate outcomes (delayed recall, calibration), then long-term outcomes (course exam grades, NCLEX readiness). This is the Tier 4 artifact and the Digital Promise submission.
- **Design ladder for a small sample.**
  1. **Within-subject designs** (best at small N). Randomize *items or concepts* within each student to Cordia's spaced and retrieval mode versus a reread or summary-only condition. Then test both on a **delayed retention** quiz 1–2 weeks later. Each student is their own control, which multiplies statistical power because there are many items per person.
  2. **A/B tests of features** (for example, format-switched retries on vs. off; Leitner vs. FSRS). These randomize users or sessions and are useful for product decisions. Report them as internal experiments, not efficacy.
  3. **Correlational with controls (Tier 3 path).** Link study behavior to Canvas grades (Cordia's planned feedback loop) and control for prior GPA or prior exam score. This needs a comparison group and an institution's cooperation.
  4. **Quasi-experimental or RCT (Tier 2/1).** Section-level comparisons at one or more nursing programs. ESSA Tier 1–2 require large, multisite samples, so these are not realistic in year 1.
- **Power reality.** With 8 weekly actives, a between-groups study cannot detect plausible effects. Recruit a bounded cohort, for example one or two USA nursing or pre-nursing course sections, and use within-subject item randomization.
- **IRB and partner.** Any study publishing results on students' data needs IRB review through a faculty principal investigator. A University of South Alabama faculty member in nursing, psychology or education is the natural partner, and course-level data access requires institutional approval. (USA-specific IRB procedures were not researched here.)
- **Pre-registration.** Post hypotheses, primary outcome (delayed retention score), and analysis plan on OSF before data collection. Report all outcomes, including nulls.
- **Third-party evaluators (published prices).**
  - LearnPlatform by Instructure: Evidence Subscription (one ESSA Level II–III study) $75,000 per year; Validation of up to 3 existing studies $30,000 per year (2023 AWS Marketplace listings).
  - LXD Research: Tier IV package $7,500–$12,500 (4–8 weeks); look-back Tier II/III study $15,000–$35,000+ (4–6 months); real-time Tier I/II study $40,000–$80,000+.
  - Empirical Education and WestEd pricing was not found in this research.

## Claims Cordia can and can't make at each stage

**The legal standard.** The FTC requires "competent and reliable evidence" for efficacy and accuracy claims. Its recent AI and edtech orders show how it applies that standard:
- **DoNotPay (finalized Feb 2025):** $193,000 in relief and a ban on claiming its AI performs like a lawyer without evidence. The complaint stressed the company "did not test" its "AI lawyer" against human lawyers.
- **Workado (final order Aug 2025):** barred from advertising AI-detection accuracy (it had claimed "98 percent") without competent and reliable evidence. It must retain that evidence.
- **Illuminate Education (announced Dec 2025, final order 2026):** barred from misrepresenting privacy and security practices. It must delete unnecessary student data and obtain biennial third-party assessments for 10 years.

Relevance to Cordia: AI-accuracy claims ("zero hallucination"), professional-substitution claims ("like a tutor or nurse educator") and privacy and security promises are all squarely enforceable.

| Stage | Can say | Can't say (yet) |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** (no study, no badge) | "Built on learning-science principles such as spaced review and retrieval practice" (cite sources); "Every question is checked against your source material" *if* you can show the check exists and log failures; "Not affiliated with or endorsed by NCSBN" | "Evidence-based," "proven," "clinically/scientifically proven"; "improves grades/scores"; "zero hallucinations" as an absolute (say "designed to…" plus a measured error rate once you have one); any pass rate; "guaranteed pass"; "official NCLEX prep"; "NCSBN-approved" |
| **After Digital Promise Tier 4** | "Earned Digital Promise's Research-Based Design: ESSA Tier 4 product certification"; "Research-based design" | Imply the certification shows efficacy. Digital Promise itself says it is "not an endorsement of products or their efficacy" |
| **After the pre-registered retention study** | "In a pre-registered study of N students at [institution], items studied with Cordia were recalled X% more after Y days than items [reread]," with the comparison, sample and limitations stated | Generalize to exam scores or pass rates; claim "students score higher" |
| **After a Tier 3 study** | "ESSA Tier 3 (promising) evidence: use associated with higher [outcome], controlling for [prior achievement]" | Causal language ("causes," "raises"); Tier 2/1 language |
| **Pass rates** | Only from a defined, complete cohort with a verifiable method, disclosed denominator and comparison | Self-selected testimonials presented as rates; national-average comparisons without controls |
| **Guarantees** | A clearly stated money-back policy with conditions disclosed up front | "Guaranteed to pass" |

**Evidence-based vs. research-based.** Under ESSA, "evidence-based" is a defined term covering Tiers 1–4. Tier 4 technically counts, but buyers read "evidence-based" as tested efficacy, so Cordia should reserve it until it has Tier 3 or better. "Research-based design" accurately describes products grounded in learning-science literature. This is a judgment about buyer expectations, rated moderate.

**Trademark-safe naming.** Use NCLEX®, MCAT®, LSAT®, GRE®, SAT®, ACT® only descriptively ("practice questions for NCLEX-RN® preparation"). Never put them in product names, logos or domains, and add an ownership and non-affiliation line. The AAMC terms expressly require "the appropriate registration symbol and statement" of ownership, and ETS has formal informational-use guidelines. Avoid "Official," which is effectively reserved for exam owners' own products and licensees ("Official LSAT Content Licensee"). Rename the feature from "NCLEX practice" to "NCLEX®-style practice," and label generated items as "original practice questions, not actual exam questions."

## Caveats

- Several prices are not public or not verified: AICPA and NCBE license fees, Instructure paid-tier fees, Digital Promise AI certification fee, 1EdTech membership, SOC 2, Empirical Education, WestEd, and ACT and College Board licensing terms. The iKeepSafe $6,400 figure comes from an undated founder blog and LearnPlatform prices are 2023 listings, so both may have changed.
- Digital Promise's ~8-week review time is from 2020.
- The adoption analysis for nursing is based on university fee pages, not interviews with deans. Law school and general-university academic-success adoption was thinly sourced.
- Third-party "rankings" and blogs were used only for corroboration, not as primary evidence.

## What Cordia should do

1. **Treat retention as the evidence project.** Ship a "next exam" onboarding: connect Canvas, pick the next exam, get a diagnostic and a daily plan. Track D1/D7/D30 cohorts weekly. Do not buy any certification above $1,000 until weekly actives reach the low hundreds.
2. **Build a "Grounding Ledger."** For every generated question, store the source span it cites and run an automated support check. Publish the measured unsupported-item rate monthly. This turns "zero hallucination" from a liability into a substantiated, FTC-defensible accuracy claim, and it is excellent evidence for Responsibly Designed AI.
3. **Publish `/research` and `/trust` pages this month.** The research page carries the logic model, annotated bibliography and design rationale. The trust page carries privacy, security, AI-use, data deletion, the Honor Code, instructor opt-out, a completed HECVAT 4 download and a VPAT.
4. **Apply for Digital Promise Research-Based Design: ESSA Tier 4 ($650)** after running 8–12 recorded user interviews as the qualitative study.
5. **Request a Common Sense Privacy evaluation** and fix whatever it flags. Remove any mention of the Student Privacy Pledge.
6. **Email two or three USA faculty** (College of Nursing; psychology/learning sciences) proposing a co-authored, IRB-approved, OSF-pre-registered within-subject delayed-retention study, with Cordia providing the platform and data exports at no cost.
7. **Build an in-product experiment harness:** randomize items to conditions, schedule delayed quizzes, export de-identified data. Use it for both research and A/B testing (FSRS vs. Leitner).
8. **Make the NCLEX features trademark-safe now.** Rename to "NCLEX®-style practice," add an NCSBN non-affiliation footer, and add a content-provenance rule that rejects pasted "recalled exam" material. Align item tagging to the published NCSBN 2026 RN Test Plan categories (descriptive use only).
9. **Join the Instructure partner program at the free tier** and plan LTI 1.3. Before applying for paid API access, budget for SOC 2 or Instructure's security assessment.
10. **Position for nursing programs as an ATI complement:** $29 per semester, department or donor-subsidized cohort pilots, and reporting to faculty on student engagement. Defer licensed-content exams; when ready, LSAT is the only one with public, per-seat licensing ($38 per student).
