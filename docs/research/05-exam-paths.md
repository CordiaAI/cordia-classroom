# Cordia Classroom: Verified Prep-Path Research for 12 High-Stakes Exams (November 2026)

Cordia should build its first premade paths for the LSAT, the bar exam (NextGen UBE + MPRE) and the ACT, because those exam owners run paid, published routes for third parties to license official content. NCLEX-RN/PN should come next as an original-content path with the best Canvas fit. SAT and MCAT paths should use only original content and point students to official materials, because College Board does not license SAT materials for commercial use and AAMC limits prep companies to bulk purchase of access codes.

## TL;DR
- **Licensing decides build order more than demand does.** LSAC publishes a per-student license ($38; $19 for nonprofits offering free prep). NCBE licenses retired MBE questions to bar-prep companies, including UWorld ("1,150+ questions licensed directly from the NCBE"), TestMax/BarMax ("1,950+ official NCBE-licensed MBE practice questions") and JD Advising. ACT runs a strategic-partner program with official questions. College Board says SAT materials "are NOT available for commercial license," and AAMC lets prep companies only bulk-buy access codes.
- **The strongest signal across all 12 exams is the first-attempt/repeat gap.** NCLEX-RN 2025: 86.7% first-time vs 52.7% repeat (U.S.-educated). FE first-time 61–69% vs repeat 25–37%. USMLE Step 1 US MD: 93% vs 71%. Bar 2025: 76% of first-timers. Cordia's promise should be "be ready the first time," built around readiness gates and the test date.
- **Recent format changes make older materials outdated.** 2026 NCLEX test plan (April 1, 2026; titles and activity statements only). LSAT dropped Logic Games (August 2024) and moved in-center (August 2026). NextGen UBE launched July 28–29, 2026. The Enhanced ACT (2025) made Science optional. The GRE runs 1 hr 58 min. Every path needs a format-version field and content-expiry check.

## Key Findings (cross-exam)

| Finding | Evidence strength | Implication for Cordia |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice beats rereading | Strong. Replicated over decades (Dunlosky et al., 2013 review; Adesope et al., 2017 meta-analysis — foundational, still the main evidence). | Learn mode's "only recall masters" rule is well grounded. |
| Spaced beats massed practice; best gap grows with retention interval | Strong (Cepeda et al., 2006 meta-analysis, foundational). | Key Flashcards intervals to days remaining before the Canvas/test date. |
| Interleaving similar problem types improves discrimination | Moderate. Brunmair & Richter's 2019 meta-analysis (Psychological Bulletin; 59 studies, 238 effect sizes) found a moderate overall effect (g = 0.42) and g = 0.34 for math, but a secondary summary reports blocking was better for word lists (g = −0.39). | Interleave in Practice/Quiz after a topic is "familiar." |
| High-confidence errors are corrected well after feedback (hypercorrection) | Moderate. Butterfield & Metcalfe (2001) found "highly confident errors were the most likely to be corrected in a subsequent retest," and the effect held after a 1-week delay (Butterfield & Mangels, 2003); field evidence is thinner. | Treat "Sure + wrong" as priority re-queue and calibration signal. |
| Full-length timed practice is diagnostic and builds stamina | Moderate. LSAC: "research shows that taking full practice tests is the most effective way to prepare for the LSAT." | Every path ends with official or high-fidelity timed mocks. |
| First attempts pass far more often than repeats | Strong (NCSBN, NCEES, USMLE, NCBE statistics). Partly selection: weaker candidates repeat. | Readiness gates before booking; a repeat path that changes method. |
| Content decays after training ends | Moderate. NCLEX 2025 first-time U.S.-educated: 89.1% (Apr–Jun) vs 81.2% (Oct–Dec). FE takers before graduation pass at 71.3% (secondary, citing NCEES). | Nudge students via texting assistant to test soon after program completion. |

**Popular beliefs the evidence does not support**
- *Learning styles*: meshing studies repeatedly failed (Pashler et al., 2008, foundational). Do **not** ask learning style in onboarding; ask behaviors (reread vs self-test, cram vs space).
- *"The 2026 NCLEX is harder"*: all eight category percentages and the 0.00-logit RN standard were kept.
- *"Logic Games are still worth studying"*: removed August 2024.
- *Vendor pass claims*: Kaplan's own footnote shows its NCLEX-RN figure rests on self-reported post-exam surveys (August 2023–March 2026) of 324 first-time takers, of whom 307 (95%) passed — a self-selected sample, so weak evidence.
- *"More questions always = better"*: no exam owner publishes a validated question-count threshold. Volume targets here are practitioner conventions (weak) unless stated.

## 1. NCLEX-RN

**Narrative summary.** Owned by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN), delivered by Pearson VUE. It is a computerized adaptive test (CAT) of 85–150 items in 5 hours, with Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) items: three 6-item case studies, bowtie, matrix, drag-and-drop, highlight and cloze, plus standalone clinical-judgment items, with partial credit on some items. The 2026 test plan (April 1, 2026 – March 31, 2029) kept all eight Client Needs percentages, renamed "Safety and Infection Control" to "Safety and Infection Prevention and Control," and refined activity statements (health equity, internal monitoring devices, social-media confidentiality). The RN standard remains 0.00 logits. Registration is $200, rising to $350 for registrations on or after Feb 1, 2027.

2025 NCSBN data: first-time U.S.-educated 86.7% (167,272 of 192,916), down from 91.2% in 2024; repeat U.S.-educated 52.7%; first-time internationally educated 47.3%; all candidates 69.1%. Some secondary sites misstate 69.1% as the first-time rate.

**Prep and mistakes.** NCSBN publishes no hours or question counts. Practitioner norms (weak) are about 6–10 weeks post-graduation with daily NGN practice and several CAT readiness exams. Mistakes: delaying the test; drilling only standalone MC; passive rationale reading; reading CAT shut-off length as a pass/fail signal.

**Licensing, partnership, trademark.** NCLEX®, NCLEX-RN®, NCLEX-PN® and NCLEX Practice Exam® are registered NCSBN marks; test plans "may not be used or reproduced without written permission" except by nonprofit educators. No general prep-partner program, but NurseAchieve hosts the NCSBN Interactive Sample Pack "used under license from NCSBN" while disclaiming association. Industry norm: "NCLEX® is a registered trademark of NCSBN… not authorized, sponsored or endorsed by NCSBN."

**Competitors.** Archer RN Q-Bank + CAT (3,100+ Qs): $79/1 mo, $129/3 mo, $149/6 mo. Kaplan: pass guarantee and a self-reported survey claim (307 of 324 respondents, 95%). Nexclex: from $9.99/mo.

## 3. MCAT

Mistake: treating third-party full-length scores as predictive. **Competitors:** Blueprint self-paced $999; UWorld QBank about $339; Jack Westin JW+ $29.99/mo; budget AAMC + UWorld about $663.

## 4. LSAT

**Narrative summary.** Owned by LSAC. Since August 2024: two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension, one unscored variable section, plus separate Argumentative Writing; LSAC found dropping Logic Games "had virtually no impact on overall scoring." From August 2026 most candidates test at Prometric centers on the LawHub interface. Scale 120–180; percentiles not gathered here. **Materials and licensing:** LawHub free (four PrepTests); LawHub Advantage $124/yr (LSAC's licensing page still lists $120). The **Coaching license is $38/student ($19 nonprofit free prep)** for "official LSAT questions and disclosed tests in your prep offerings"; a **Public Marketing license is $5,000/yr**. LSAC lists "Official LSAT Content Licensees." **Prep:** full timed PrepTests are LSAC's top recommendation; studying Logic Games is the commonest avoidable mistake. **Competitors:** 7Sage from $69/mo; Magoosh $199/12 mo; self-study subscriptions about $65–$150/mo; all plus Advantage, a common complaint.

## 5. Bar exam: legacy UBE, NextGen UBE and MPRE

**Narrative summary.** NCBE develops the exams; jurisdictions administer them, grade written work and set cut scores. **Legacy UBE** (through February 2028): MBE (200 MC, 175 scored), MEE (six essays), two MPTs; cut scores about 260–270. **NextGen UBE:** first given July 28–29, 2026 to 2,640 examinees in 10 jurisdictions (CT, Guam, ID, MD, MO, N. Mariana Islands, OR, Palau, USVI, WA); nine hours over 1.5 days; computer-delivered; 500–750 scale; early cut scores 610–620. 13 more jurisdictions join July 2027, and "52 jurisdictions will begin administering the NextGen UBE between now and July 2028." It combines MC questions, integrated question sets and performance tasks. One secondary site reports a 72.7% July 2026 pass rate; unverified, so do not use until NCBE publishes. **Pass rates (2025, NCBE):** 76% of first-timers (July 79%, February 60%); 63% overall; ABA reports 84.10% for ABA-school first-timers (different population). **MPRE** national mean: 96.7 (Nov 2025), 96.3 (Aug 2025); jurisdictions set passing scores. **Licensing:** NCBE sells study aids (MBE Study Aid, 210 Qs, $100 PDF) and licenses retired questions non-exclusively: UWorld (1,150+), AdaptiBar (~1,581), TestMax (1,950+), JD Advising, SmartBarPrep. Barbri Essentials (~$1,700) reportedly uses Barbri-authored questions. **Mistakes:** under-practicing writing; using legacy-only materials in NextGen states.

## 6. CPA Exam (Core + Discipline)

**Narrative summary.** Developed by AICPA, administered with NASBA and Prometric. Three 4-hour Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) plus one Discipline (BAR, ISC or TCP). Five testlets each: two MCQ, three TBS. Counts: AUD 78/7, FAR 50/7, REG 72/8, BAR 50/7, ISC 82/6, TCP 68/7. MCQ and TBS each count half, except ISC (60/40). Pass mark 75. Core is year-round; Disciplines are in quarterly windows. - Competitor praise/complaints and many prices were not verified within this research budget; mine reviews before launch.

## What Cordia should do

**1. Build order (licensing × fit × Canvas signal).**
1. **LSAT first.** Sign LSAC's Coaching license ($38/student) and require or link LawHub Advantage. Skill-based content fits Learn/Quiz/Practice with confidence-tagged blind review.
2. **Bar (NextGen UBE + MPRE) second.** License NCBE retired questions. Build NextGen first, since legacy content expires after February 2028. AI grading in Practice mode suits integrated sets and performance tasks, which MC-centric banks under-serve. Branch by jurisdiction cut score.
3. **NCLEX-RN/PN third (original content).** Best Canvas fit; Cordia already has NCLEX practice. Write original NGN items mapped to the 2026 plan; approach NCSBN about the Sample Pack (NurseAchieve precedent). Add a PN repeat-taker path (41.6% repeat pass rate).
4. **ACT fourth.** Apply to ACT Strategic Partners; this is the high-school wedge.
5. **FE and CPA fifth.** Original content plus free NCEES/AICPA materials and handbook-lookup drills; explore AICPA licensing directly.
6. **MCAT, Step 1, GRE, Praxis, SAT later, as original-only companion paths.** Link to the owner's products (AAMC bundle, CBSSA, POWERPREP, ETS practice, Bluebook/Khan) and ingest only the student's own score reports. Never ingest official SAT items.

**2. Path structure (all exams).**
- **Phase 0, Diagnostic (5–10%).** Map items to blueprint categories; capture accuracy and confidence. "Sure + wrong" is the top remediation signal.
- **Phase 1, Recall-first repair (~40%).** Learn-mode ladder weighted by blueprint percentage × weakness; spacing keyed to days remaining.
- **Phase 2, Application, interleaved (30–40%).** Exam-format items; misses return in a different format.
- **Phase 3, Simulation and readiness gate (15–25%).** Timed full-lengths, official where allowed (LawHub, AAMC, POWERPREP, CBSSA, Bluebook) via student-side links. Gate the "book your exam" nudge on a readiness threshold met on two consecutive mocks.
- **Repeat-taker variant.** Re-diagnose, change method (more retrieval and calibration, less rereading) and show the official first/repeat gap honestly.

**3. Question volumes.** Do not hard-code "X,000 questions." Set coverage targets: every blueprint subcategory mastered, at least two spaced successful recalls per idea, and exam-format items per category proportional to weight. Use the Canvas feedback loop to learn per-student volume needs.

**4. Onboarding.** Ask behavioral questions ("When you review, do you mostly reread or test yourself?"; "Do you study in one long session before tests?"). Do **not** ask about learning styles.

**5. Marketing-safe naming.**
- Use Cordia-owned names: "Cordia Nursing Licensure Path — for the NCLEX-RN®," "Cordia Law Admissions Path — for the LSAT®." Keep the exam mark descriptive, visually subordinate, and out of product/company names, domains and metatags (apply College Board's strict rules globally).
- Add a disclaimer: "[MARK]® is a registered trademark of [owner], which is not affiliated with and does not endorse Cordia."
- Say "official" or "licensed questions" only under a signed license (LSAC, NCBE, ACT). Never use owner logos.
- Publish no pass guarantees or "X% of Cordia students pass" claims until outcome data is audited with disclosed denominators.

**6. Format-version guardrails.** Store `format_version` and an expiry date on every item, and auto-flag content when owners change formats: NCLEX plan ends March 31, 2029; legacy UBE ends February 2028; Enhanced ACT; post-2024 LSAT.
